Overall finding

The correspondence supports a clear distinction between the two registration files. The 4 November letter is headed for title WA231076 and answers the seven numbered questions sent on 12 August. It identifies the Form 1E as the location of the “question or doubt” statement and refers back to a retained-document list for the other numbered points.

The 15 October correspondence made a separate set of requests about WA240304 and the 1983 first-registration process. In section 3.6 it asked for each notice and service record, with the title, addressee, address, date and proof of delivery. Although the 4 November letter refers to the 15 October correspondence, it does not provide those particulars or state whether the relevant notice or service record exists.

Conclusion on notice: the date and manner of notification to an occupier are not established by the material assessed here. Silence in the 4 November reply is not proof that no notice was sent.

Response-by-response assessment

1. The 12 August seven-point enquiry

  • Question 1 — 1971 Abstract of Title: the 4 November letter points to the retained-document list. It does not itself state whether the abstract survives, which file it belongs to or its item number.
  • Question 2 — “question or doubt” statement: a direct answer is given: HMLR says the statement is in the Form 1E application. That answer identifies a document but does not identify the corresponding Form 1C for WA240304.
  • Question 3 — other interests and disputes: the reply points to the document list rather than giving a direct yes/no response about the named family members, occupation, objections or disputes recorded in the file.
  • Questions 4 and 7 — surviving documents and OC2 item numbers: the letter relies on the previously supplied list. The specific list, not this short letter, must be used to verify the item numbers and any gaps.
  • Question 5 — historic parcel identity: no direct comparison is given between the historic farmhouse/curtilage records and the parcel in the registration application.
  • Question 6 — 1989 Certificate A: no direct response is given. The question was framed as a request to identify what was before the Registrar in 1982–83, not to decide the later certificate’s legal effect.

2. The 15 October WA240304 request

The 15 October request was framed as a factual records and document-identification enquiry, including the missing 1969 deed, the 1971 abstract, Form 1C, D23 examination notes, the 1983 letters, plans, notice/service records, the 1987 amalgamation and the 1994 correction applications. The 4 November letter does not answer those items one by one. It says no application for alteration or indemnity may be considered, cites the 23 March, 19 April and 25 May letters, and directs further action to the Court.

The Court/indemnity point does not itself identify what documents are held or what the 1983 file records. A request for copies, item numbers and service records is distinct from asking HMLR to give legal advice or determine ownership.

3. What the Stage 2 position does and does not establish

The 9 October 2026 ICR letter quotes the 25 May Stage 2 response as saying HMLR had not received or been aware of a family interest, was unaware of a possible ongoing dispute and had received no caution application. That is a statement about the Registry’s recorded knowledge and the application history as described by HMLR. It is not a notice register, a copy of a notice, or proof of service on Mary Williams or any other occupier.

The ICR letter also refers to the Stage 1 response as dated 19 April 2025. The archive contains an HMLR delivery email dated 19 April 2026 for a document titled “Stage one complaint decision”. The dateline on the underlying PDF must be checked to determine whether these are separate responses or a one-year date discrepancy; the delivery email alone does not settle that issue.

The ICR letter characterises the 1987 Court of Appeal decision as a substantive ruling on ownership. This review records that as the ICR’s position; it does not treat that characterisation as an answer to the separate question of what the Registry received, examined or sent in 1982–83.

4. Separate subject access request D260928

HMLR asked for identity evidence on 27 October. On 2 November it offered email, post or password-protected documents as ways to lodge the evidence. The requester supplied a password-protected identity/proof-of-address PDF on 2 November and sent the password separately on 3 November. Those sensitive attachments are deliberately excluded from the public evidence page. The 4 November Z084NQV letter is not a response to D260928 and should not be treated as one.

Audit scope and limitations

The 73-row register below is the Wayback index snapshot generated on 30 October 2026. It distinguishes HMLR, ICR and mixed-agency records; it is not correct to describe all 73 as direct HMLR letters. The archive includes links to message text and to attachments where the index records them.

The 4 November letter was visually inspected from the recipient-supplied one-page PDF and transcribed for this record. The 9 October ICR letter was read as a separate source. The older HMLR PDF attachments dated 23 March, 19 April, 25 May, 13 July and 30 July are linked below, but their full contents have not all been independently re-read in this particular assessment. Their inclusion in the index is not treated as proof that every attached page has been checked.

This assessment therefore updates the correspondence position through 4 November 2026 but remains a working forensic review, not a completed page-by-page audit of every historic attachment. It establishes no conclusion that a notice was not sent, that a registration was invalid, or that any person acted improperly.

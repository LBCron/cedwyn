What the source states (pitch page read 2026-11-05)

Drafting the Past (draftingthepast.com) is "a platform dedicated to nonfiction history books". It publishes reviews, essays and interviews, runs a podcast, and has a subscriber newsletter. Its "Pitch Us" page (draftingthepast.com) sets out three separate routes, all to [address kept in the project workspace]: - BOOK TO REVIEW (subject line). Authors or publishers pitch a book; "if it is a good potential fit, we will reply with an ARC request". - Timing is the hard rule: pitch at least three months before publication. They publish reviews up to six weeks after publication. In September 2026 they were taking pitches for books out in Dec 2026 / Jan 2027 or later. - They take nonfiction history only, not fiction; university-press books are welcome, but "rarely" purely academic ones. - PODCAST (subject line). A guest pitch, after reading the host Kate's own advice on pitching the podcast (linked from that page). - BOOK REVIEW (subject line). This route is for writers who want to review for them: paid $200 per single-book review (1,000 words max, newspaper style), more for review essays (1,500 words max). - House rules: no gen-AI content, and contributors sign a contract saying so. No unsolicited attachments; they are deleted. No reply within two weeks means a decline. Their words: "we get FAR more pitches than we have space for coverage". They also ask pitchers to write a specific check phrase in the email, as a way to catch bulk or automated pitches. - The site also publishes monthly "new history books we're looking forward to" lists, e.g. ten picks for September 2026 (search result, not read in full), which works as a listing-style target with the same lead time.

What is not checked: no source gives reach or sales per review or podcast episode. Their audience looks mostly US (US examples and outlets); no UK-specific reach figure was found.

How to use it (checklist)

  1. Fit test before anything: the book has to be nonfiction history, written for general readers, with a publication date at least 3 months away.
  2. Pitch text: who you are, the book, the release date, and why it fits their readers. No attachments, a link only. Include their check phrase.
  3. Calendar: put the pitch date at pub date minus 3+ months, and a "no reply means no" date 2 weeks later. Never chase.
  4. The podcast route doesn't depend on a release date. It needs a hook ("what this record shows about...") and the host's pitching advice read first.
  5. Gen-AI rule: any text sent to them, and any guest material, has to be the author's own words. Pitch drafts written by an assistant have to be rewritten or approved by the author as their own, or not sent.

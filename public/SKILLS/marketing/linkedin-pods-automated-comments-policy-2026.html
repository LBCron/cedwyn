LinkedIn: engagement pods and automated comments (2026 policy) and what to do instead

Harvested 2026-11-04 (skills harvest). Topic gap: 'automated comment' 0 hits and 'engagement pod' 0 hits in INDEX.md. Companion to linkedin-requests-posts-heritage.md and linkedin-2026-format-benchmarks-document-vs-image-vs-video.md (formats/requests are covered there).

What LinkedIn states (primary source, read 2026-11-04)

LinkedIn Pressroom, "What We're Doing to Support Authentic Content and Conversations on LinkedIn" (13 Mar 2026): - Engagement pods = "a coordinated group of people that like, comment and share posts to attempt to boost visibility". Not allowed. LinkedIn has removed groups showing pod behaviour and warned members, including threatened removal from programmes such as Top Voices and possible account restrictions. - Automated comments = comments posted "using a browser extension, script, or third party tool" without a human clicking Comment. Not allowed. If detected LinkedIn may hide them from "Most Relevant" comments, sometimes show them only inside the commenter's network, and restrict the account. - Suspected inauthentic engagement "may affect how that content is distributed" (reach cut on the poster, not only the commenter). - Verification: 100M+ members have at least one verification; Page verification now for companies with a Premium Company Page. A LinkedIn trust-and-safety post (Gyanda, Nov 2025, search snippet) states the goal "to make engagement pods ineffective"; a quoted LinkedIn line (Aug-Sep 2025, snippet): "If we detect excessive comment creation or use of an automation tool, we may limit the visibility of those comments."

Practitioner numbers (context)

  • Richard van der Blom, Algorithm InSights 2025 (LinkedIn post + AuthoredUp summary, snippets): average organic reach down ~34-50% in 2025; mobile 72% of sessions; the first 30-60 minutes of genuine engagement and saves drive later reach. 2026 edition covers 1.3M posts (Creator Science podcast page).
  • Read: when reach is scarce, a pod or a bot looks tempting, and that is exactly what LinkedIn now detects and punishes; the cost lands on the whole profile.

Do / don't

Don't - Join pods, "comment-for-comment" groups, WhatsApp/Telegram boost rings. - Post comments on LinkedIn from any script, browser automation or scheduler tool (posting your OWN post via the official share flow is not a comment; commenting must be a human click). - Mass-comment the same template ("Great insight!") across many posts in a short burst ("excessive comment creation").

Do - Write 2-5 substantive comments a day by hand on posts from heritage, archive, Welsh history and local-press people, each adding a fact or source (comments are content and build profile visits). - Reply to every comment on our own posts within the first hour, by hand. - Ask genuine partners (Welsh history societies, archive staff we already correspond with) to share when a post is truly relevant to them; no reciprocal-engagement deals. - Add a profile verification if available free (workplace/ID) as a trust signal.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • The saved LinkedIn login ("Cedwyn posting") may be used for posting our own content only. No scripted LinkedIn comments, ever: engage.py currently targets YouTube/Instagram/Facebook only; keep LinkedIn out of it and note this rule in the project workspace at the next edit (proposal; this run edited nothing).
  • Same logic applies by analogy to Meta and YouTube (Meta demotes bait comments since Nov 2018; see facebook-engagement-bait-cta-wording.md): keep engage.py caps (3/platform/day, 45 min apart) and unique, source-quoting text per comment; never template comments.
  • Any LinkedIn comment work is a human task: draft candidate comments into HubSpot for the project lead to post himself if he wants, with the source line included.
  • Never join or form a pod with other agents or accounts in the hive.

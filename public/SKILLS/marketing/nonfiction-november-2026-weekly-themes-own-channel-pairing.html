Nonfiction November 2026: the reading event running now, and how a regional history e-book joins in on its own channels

Date: 2026-11-05 · Harvest run. "Nonfiction November" had 0 hits in skill files before this run (1 passing mention in the Perlego file).

What it is (read 2026-11-05)

  • A month-long reader event every November, with two main strands: 1. The blog strand, hosted across 4 blogs with one theme a week (read on booksaremyfavouriteandbest.com, 29 Oct 2026, and chapteradventure.com):
    • Wk1 Nov 2-8 "My Year in Nonfiction" (Based on a True Story)
    • Wk2 Nov 9-15 "Nonfiction Journeys" (Volatile Rune)
    • Wk3 Nov 16-22 "Nonfiction Pairings" (Adventures in Reading, Running and Working from Home)
    • Wk4 Nov 23-29 "What I've Added to my TBR" (Readerbuzz) 2. The BookTube/StoryGraph strand, hosted by abookolive. It has four optional one-word prompts: root, puzzle, sample, frame. The StoryGraph challenge page showed 38 participants and 43 books in a snippet (not checked live).
  • BookTube reach this week (yt-dlp read, 2026-11-05): abookolive "Is Nonfiction in Trouble? | The State of Nonfiction in 2026" had 26,977 views. Steve Donoghue's #nonfictionnovember videos had 2,122 and 3,661 views. RedheadReading's "Nonfiction November TBR" had 373, and the same channel did a Book Chat covering Welsh history.
  • One participant's comment: "I somehow manage to finish Nonfiction November with a bunch of books added to the TBR stack". This is the point of the event: readers are actively looking for nonfiction to add.
  • Tag in use: #NonfictionNovember on Instagram, YouTube and blogs. The figure #nonfictionnovember 2026 posts is not checked (Instagram doesn't publish tag counts).

The play for a small publisher (no outreach needed)

  1. Post on your own channels with the tag and the week's theme. Reader events reward taking part, not ads.
  2. Week 3 "Pairings" is the best fit for a niche title: pair a well-known book with yours. For example, John Davies' A History of Wales goes with a one-parish deep dive.
  3. The "root" prompt (abookolive) fits a place/family-roots history.
  4. Keep the reading invitation free and easy, with a free edition and one link. The event is about reading, and a sales pitch reads as off-key.
  5. Commenting on host blogs or link-up lists counts as engagement with strangers. Under our rules it is a proposal, not an action.

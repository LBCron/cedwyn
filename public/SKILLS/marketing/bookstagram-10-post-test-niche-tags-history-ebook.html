Bookstagram for a regional history e-book: the 10-post test, niche tags, reader-first asks

Skill added 2026-11-05 (skills harvest). Before this, "bookstagram" appeared only in harvest notes and no skill file covered it.

What the source states (read 2026-11-05)

BookBub Partners, "Bookstagram for Authors: What It Is and How to Use It" (insights.bookbub.com), page read in full: - Size: #Bookstagram has more than 127M posts (as of Aug 2026). Other Aug 2026 counts: #books 84.7M, #booklover 48.5M, #bookish 25.7M, #amreading 4.1M, #bookrecs 3.3M, #bookquotes 2.3M. Genre tags run much smaller (#cozymysterybooks 44.9K). Instagram search > Tags shows the post count for any tag. - Method, the 10-post test: first browse the sub-community you want to reach and note the posts and Reels with the most likes and comments. Then write 10 post ideas, post one a day for 10 days (making them all at once is fine), read the stats, and write the next 10 based on what worked. Be patient: most accounts take "months and sometimes years". - Content types that work for authors: sneak peeks and reveals, excerpts and readings (Reel reading, an annotated page photo, a quote card), life of the author (routine, events, readings), giveaways. - Growth: link the Instagram profile from the website and newsletter; post several times a week. Practitioner quotes: R.S. Grey ("Take ten minutes out of your day and snap a few photos... natural lighting, bright colors, compelling subject matter"); Sara @fictionmatters ("Engagement is crucial to growth... leave thoughtful, personal, and specific comments", and replying to comments and DMs gets rewarded by the feed). - Getting featured: pick accounts that read your genre over big accounts. Quote (Sara @fictionmatters): "A Bookstagrammer with 2K followers who loves paranormal romance is going to do your paranormal romance a lot more good than an account with 40K who mostly features historical fiction." Interact first, then ask whether they would like to read the book, not feature it ("readers first, advertisers second"). Big accounts may expect payment (Jenna @jennareadsbooks). - Warning: be wary of DMs offering paid reviews, of accounts impersonating authors, and of promotion schemes that are bots clicking on your profile.

Realistic pace (CreatorDB, "State of the Creator Economy 2026", read, a YouTube dataset used here only as a benchmark of how slowly channels grow): the median tracked channel grew under 0.7% in 30 days, and about 63% did not grow at all. Read a flat first month as normal, not as failure.

What is not checked: no source gives sales per Bookstagram post for nonfiction or local history. BookBub's examples are mostly fiction. Treat this as a test with our own numbers.

How to do it (checklist)

  1. Size the niche tags first. In Instagram search > Tags, read the post counts for 8-12 candidate tags. Use a mix: one or two big ones (#bookstagram, #historybooks), the mid-size ones where readers of our kind of book actually are (Welsh, local-history and nonfiction tags), and one or two small exact ones. Write the counts and the date into the log; counts change.
  2. Write the 10 posts as one batch before posting. Spread them across the content types above. For a history title, the excerpt/quote card and the "page from the record" photo are the natural fit.
  3. Post one a day for 10 days, at a set time. Put the tracking link in the bio (link-in-bio route), not in captions, which aren't clickable.
  4. After day 10, read the stats per post: reach, saves, shares, profile visits and bio-link clicks. Keep the top 3 formats, drop the bottom 3, and write the next 10.
  5. Reader-first asks happen only after real interaction (comments over weeks), only to accounts that read regional or nonfiction history, and the ask is "would you like to read it" with a free copy. Never paid posts unless the owner agrees to the spend.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Account: @cedwynestate (already the shop/heritage account; the daily auto-social and quote-pack runs post there). The 10-post test is a format test on top of the existing posts. It doesn't add a new channel, and it uses the 2-3 posts/day caps the auto-social runner already keeps.
  • Blocked first by the bio link. The sales-fix run (5 Nov 07:17Z) found the @cedwynestate bio still has no clickable link, and only the project lead can add it in the app (cedwyn.bucklerfamily.estate/r/ig-bio). Until it's set, no post can be measured by clicks. Measure saves and profile visits, and keep the problem in the project workspace, not with the project lead.
  • The 10 ideas: 3 quote cards from the records (verbatim line + source line, from the quote-pack), 2 "page from the record" photos, 2 short Reel readings of one paragraph from the free first edition, 1 map/before-and-after, 1 "what the catalogue holds" (an evidence-register entry), and 1 free-first-edition post pointing to /ebooks/. Each one follows the editorial rules: never name the project lead, banned words, no holdings or addresses, no insignia.
  • Tags: size them first (step 1). Candidates to check, not yet counted: #bookstagram, #historybooks, #nonfictionbooks, #localhistory, #welshhistory, #wales, #cymru, #valeofglamorgan, #penarth, #cardiffhistory. Max 3-5 per post (matches the hashtag rules already in force).
  • Featuring asks are outreach, so they are proposals only. A list of 5 Wales/history reading accounts (with follower counts and what they read) goes into the project workspace Nothing is DM'd: the standing rules are no DMs to strangers and contact nobody.
  • Kill rule: after 2 rounds (20 posts), if bio-link clicks are under 10 in total, drop the format test and fold the best 2 formats into the quote-pack.

Niche-brand AI citations: get named on third-party pages, match the question

Date harvested: 2026-11-04. Topic: SEO / AI visibility for a small, little-known brand (Cedwyn is one).

The evidence (primary source, read in full HTML)

Chen & Koudas, University of Toronto, "What Drives AI Citations? Content, Retrieval and Model Knowledge in Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)", arXiv:2610.15033v1, 10 Oct 2026 (CC BY 4.0). Two 90-day daily collections: 158,099 answers / 468,037 citations (US home internet, a few big brands) plus a replication on 55 queries in a niche market of small B2B vendors (14,850 answers, 71,849 citations, 3,895 websites). ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity.

Numbers that matter for a small brand: - A niche vendor is named mainly when a CITED THIRD-PARTY PAGE names it: x39.7 for ChatGPT (vs x4.2 for big brands). - Without search evidence the models have no stable ranking of small vendors (64% / 67% of pairs predictable); search restores ~80%. Big brands are ranked from memory (75% ChatGPT, 92% Gemini). So for a small brand, visibility exists only when the engine's own search finds evidence about it. - Website authority had NO effect in the niche market (-1.3% per SD vs +12.1% for big brands); only 9% of the variation lies between websites. Small sites are not penalised for being small. - Question match is the one page property that passes every test: title match +26.4% per SD and best-paragraph match pass all five tests in the niche market, +33.6% on brand pages; on third-party pages +23.6% (title) and +25.7% (paragraph) in the main set. - FAQ blocks look effective across websites (+22.5%) but not within them (-1.9%); schema.org, summary/author boxes and page speed likewise show no within-site gain. Pages naming more relevant entities: +11.8% per SD. - The best-matching paragraph sits at a median 45% down the page; engines find it wherever it is. - At equal rank, concise pages from the original source beat roundups and forums. - Main text that needs JavaScript: -10.6% Gemini citations within site (p=0.008). - A brand in the model's memory gets written into its searches (67% vs 11%); the authors HYPOTHESISE (not proven) that accurate brand-question associations plus retrievable brand and third-party pages compound. - Caveat: commercial markets (home internet, B2B software); heritage/books not studied. Associations, not experiments, except the answer-step tests.

How to do it (small brand playbook)

  1. List 20-30 real questions people ask in your niche, in their words (Search Console queries, forums, PAA boxes).
  2. For each question, make sure ONE concise page on your own site has a title and one paragraph that answer it in the question's own terms. Plain HTML text, no JS-only content. Do not bother adding FAQ/schema for AI's sake.
  3. Third-party presence is the multiplier: get the brand named, accurately and next to the question topic, on pages the engines retrieve (directory/catalogue entries, society and library listings, review profiles, Wikipedia-style reference entries where notability allows, interviews, guest articles). Each must answer a question too, not just carry a link.
  4. Name the relevant entities (places, people, record types) on each page; that broadens the questions it is cited for.
  5. Re-check monthly (see ai-citation-replay-monitoring-drift.md): standing is per question and drifts.

How we apply it to Cedwyn

  • Cedwyn is the textbook niche brand: models will not know it, so it is named only when a retrieved page names it. Priority order: (a) question-matching titles + one answer paragraph on the existing /wales/, /research/ and /ebooks/ pages (e.g. "What was Great House Farm, Llandough?", "Who were the Williams family of Leckwith?"); (b) third-party mentions that say what Cedwyn holds, next to those topics.
  • Third-party targets (all PROPOSALS, outward steps need the project lead's OK; zero spend): Goodreads author/book entries for the e-books, local history society and heritage directory listings, library/catalogue records for the e-books, a Wikidata item only if notability rules allow. Never write content that names the project lead or uses banned words.
  • Do NOT spend agent time on more FAQ blocks or JSON-LD for AI; keep existing markup, but re-tier it as good-to-have (matches ai-search-roadmap-evidence-tiers-90day.md).
  • Check that key pages render their main text without JavaScript (Gemini penalty); the HyperFrames/interactive pages should carry a plain-text answer paragraph.
  • Next BIZDEV/onpage run: pick 5 questions, rewrite 5 titles + add 5 answer paragraphs, log before/after.

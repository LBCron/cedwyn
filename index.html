Procedural route: unresolved — filing blocked

The 1987 decision is a final Court of Appeal determination. Reopening it is not governed by the Takhar fraud test alone. CPR 52.30 requires that reopening be necessary to avoid real injustice, that the circumstances be exceptional, and that there be no alternative effective remedy.

The Court of Appeal has said the earlier process must have been critically undermined, with a powerful probability of significant injustice and a powerful probability that the decision would have been different if the process had not been undermined. A merely arguable error or fresh evidence, by itself, is not enough: Goring-on-Thames [2018] EWCA Civ 860 and Wingfield [2020] EWCA Civ 1588.

A separate Takhar action is different: it is a freestanding cause of action to set aside a judgment for fraud, not a rehearing of the appeal. It requires conscious and deliberate dishonesty attributable to the successful party (or a person for whom it is responsible), deception of the court and materiality — the fraud must have been an operative cause and must have changed how the original court approached the evidence. See Takhar v Gracefield Developments Ltd [2019] UKSC 13, Tinkler v Esken Ltd [2023] EWCA Civ 655 and RBS v Highland Financial Partners [2013] EWCA Civ 328.

Reasonable diligence is not a general prerequisite to that freestanding fraud action where fraud was not alleged at the original trial; a deliberate decision not to investigate a known suspicion may still matter to abuse of process. Diligence is therefore tracked separately, not scored as a Takhar limb.

Case-wide hard stop: the available route, the possible alternative remedy, limitation (including any deliberate-concealment issue), standing, parties, service and the application of the current rules to this historic appeal have not yet been settled by independent counsel. Until that route analysis is complete, no individual lead score — even 200/200 — makes an application ready to file.

How the score works

This is an internal evidence-readiness index, not a court-prescribed formula and not a prediction of success. Each lead is rated 0–50 on four separate, mandatory questions:

  1. Conscious, deliberate dishonesty and attribution — a specific dishonest act, statement or concealment attributable to the successful party or someone for whom it is responsible.
  2. Court deception and materiality — the court was deceived; the fraud was an operative cause of the decision and would have changed how the original court approached the evidence, not merely what might happen at a hypothetical retrial.
  3. Critical process-integrity impact — the lead is capable of showing that the earlier litigation process was critically undermined and that significant injustice probably occurred.
  4. Admissible proof and provenance — particulars, authenticated primary evidence, provenance, credible witnesses and a realistic route to admissibility.

Non-compensatory floor: 100/200 is reached only when all four limbs are at least 25/50. A high score on materiality cannot make up for no evidence of dishonesty; a strong document cannot make up for no causal link. The displayed percentage is the weakest limb divided by 25, capped at 100%, so it shows the remaining bottleneck rather than an average that can hide a missing element.

Your 200-point target: 200/200 means 50/50 on every limb — twice the internal minimum on each. This is the double-lock target before taking a proposed route forward. It is not literally 200% probability or certainty: law does not permit a numerical guarantee of how a court will decide. The case-wide route gate must also be cleared, and the complete evidence must be adversarially tested and reviewed by independent counsel.

Reasonable diligence: it is not a core limb of a freestanding Takhar fraud action where fraud was not alleged at the original trial. The Supreme Court rejected a general diligence prerequisite, while recognising that a deliberate decision not to investigate a known suspicion may raise abuse-of-process issues. Diligence remains recorded separately for route analysis and any ordinary fresh-evidence procedure.

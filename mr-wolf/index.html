Procedural route: unresolved — filing blocked

The 1987 appeal is final. Reopening it and bringing a separate fraud action are different routes; neither is assumed to be available.

Read the procedural routeCPR 52.30 · Takhar · hard stops
Reopening the final appeal · CPR 52.30

The 1987 decision is a final Court of Appeal determination. Reopening it is not governed by the Takhar fraud test alone. CPR 52.30 requires that reopening be necessary to avoid real injustice, that the circumstances be exceptional, and that there be no alternative effective remedy.

The Court of Appeal has said the earlier process must have been critically undermined, with a powerful probability of significant injustice and a powerful probability that the decision would have been different if the process had not been undermined. A merely arguable error or fresh evidence, by itself, is not enough: Goring-on-Thames [2018] EWCA Civ 860 and Wingfield [2020] EWCA Civ 1588.

A separate fraud action · Takhar

A separate Takhar action is different: it is a freestanding cause of action to set aside a judgment for fraud, not a rehearing of the appeal. It requires conscious and deliberate dishonesty attributable to the successful party (or a person for whom it is responsible), deception of the court and materiality — the fraud must have been an operative cause and must have changed how the original court approached the evidence. See Takhar v Gracefield Developments Ltd [2019] UKSC 13, Tinkler v Esken Ltd [2023] EWCA Civ 655 and RBS v Highland Financial Partners [2013] EWCA Civ 328.

Reasonable diligence

Reasonable diligence is not a general prerequisite to that freestanding fraud action where fraud was not alleged at the original trial; a deliberate decision not to investigate a known suspicion may still matter to abuse of process. Diligence is therefore tracked separately, not scored as a Takhar limb.

What remains unresolved

Case-wide hard stop: the available route, the possible alternative remedy, limitation (including any deliberate-concealment issue), standing, parties, service and the application of the current rules to this historic appeal have not yet been settled by independent counsel. How the score works

Four separate legal/evidential questions. Every gate must reach 25/50; no high score can cover a missing element.

Explore the scoring methodThreshold · 200 target · authorities
What is being tested?

The 200-point index is for a possible freestanding Takhar fraud action. It is an internal evidence-readiness model, not a court-prescribed points test, legal opinion or probability estimate. Each lead is assessed separately against four questions:

  1. Conscious and deliberate dishonesty: is there a specific dishonest act, statement or concealment, rather than mistake, negligence or mere inconsistency?
  2. Attribution and actual court deception: is the dishonesty attributable to the successful party (or a person for whom it is responsible), and was the court actually deceived?
  3. Operative cause and materiality: was the fraud an operative cause of the judgment, such that it would have changed how the original court approached the evidence?
  4. Admissible proof and provenance: are there sufficiently particularised, authenticated and admissible materials to prove the first three elements?
Minimum and progress percentage

Non-compensatory minimum: the internal floor is 25/50 on every limb. The floor is met only when all four limbs reach it; a high materiality score cannot compensate for no evidence of dishonesty, attribution or actual deception. The displayed percentage is the weakest limb divided by 25, capped at 100%. The raw total / 200 remains a separate evidence index.

The 200/200 double-lock target

Your double-lock target: 200/200 means 50/50 on every limb — twice the internal minimum on each. It is not literally 200% statistical probability and cannot guarantee a court outcome. Before any filing, independent counsel must test the full pleaded case, evidence, admissibility, defences and procedural route.

Separate procedural route · CPR 52.30

Separate route: CPR 52.30. Reopening the final Court of Appeal decision is procedurally distinct from bringing a freestanding Takhar fraud action. CPR 52.30 requires reopening to be necessary to avoid real injustice, exceptional circumstances making reopening appropriate, and no alternative effective remedy. The authorities additionally require the earlier process to have been critically undermined, a powerful probability of significant injustice and a powerful probability that the decision would have been different. Fresh evidence or an arguably wrong result alone is insufficient.

Reasonable diligence

Reasonable diligence: it is not a general prerequisite to a freestanding Takhar fraud action where fraud was not alleged at the original trial, although deliberate failure to investigate a known suspicion may be relevant to abuse of process.

National Library of Wales

4 November 2026Journal entryRefused under s.21 (reading room)ref NLW FOI/2026/16 (1610 Herbert survey S7, Bute X.6(12), 1667 lease)

Letter dated 4 November 2026 (sent 00:02 UTC on 5 November) from the National Library of Wales, signed by its Director of Collections, answering FOI/2026/16, received on 16 October 2026. The request asked in three parts whether the 1610 survey S7 of William Herbert's lands records Great House or any holding in Llandough-juxta-Penarth (folio, holder, tenure, rent, copy cost); whether Bute X.6(12), 1877 trustees' abstracts, is on the CARDMFS microfilm (reel, frame, copy cost); and whether any NLW collection holds a lease of Great House c.1660 to 1675 to a Williams or for 999 years, a later recital of it, the Bute or Windsor acquisition from the Vaughan family, or Vaughan family papers. It asked that, if section 21 was relied on, the Library say exactly where and how. Further search terms were sent on 29 October 2026.

Assessment (our reading): An on-time refusal that points to the reading room rather than answering. Section 21 is a recognised position for an archive, and the alternatives offered are useful. But the reply neither confirms nor denies holding the specific items asked about, and gives no references for where to find them, which the request expressly asked for. Those are the points a complaint to the Library's panel would test.

What they did

  • Replied within 20 working days of receipt (FOIA s.10).
  • Applied section 21 to the request as a whole: the information is reasonably accessible by visiting the Reading Room in Aberystwyth, with links to visiting and reading information.
  • Offered alternatives under its advice and assistance duty (s.16): commissioning an independent researcher from its published list, the Virtual Reading Room service, and contacting its Enquiry service about copying options.
  • Gave its complaints route (a panel of three staff not involved, chaired by a Director) and the right of appeal to the Information Commissioner.

What they did not do

  • Did not confirm or deny whether S7 contains an entry for Great House or any Llandough-juxta-Penarth holding, whether Bute X.6(12) is on the CARDMFS microfilm, or whether any 1660s lease or later recital is held. Section 21 contains no provision removing the duty to confirm or deny under s.1(1)(a).
  • Did not give item, folio, reel or frame references for where the information can be read, which the request asked for if section 21 was relied on.
  • Did not give the cost of copies of only the relevant folios or entries (Parts 1(c) and 2(b)).
  • Did not refer to the further search terms sent on 29 October 2026.
  • The email address given for the Information Commissioner in the letter is malformed.

Fair note

  • Section 21 is an absolute exemption for information reasonably accessible to the applicant by other means. Material in a public reading room is often treated as reasonably accessible, although the test looks at the applicant's own circumstances, and travel to Aberystwyth is a real cost; the Virtual Reading Room and researcher list go some way to meeting that.
  • FOIA gives a right to recorded information, not to research. Whether a 400-year-old survey contains a particular entry can require reading the manuscript, which archives commonly treat as research rather than a disclosure; the request itself framed the questions as facts to be found.
  • The reply was on time and gave practical routes, which is better than a bare refusal.

Open questions

  • Which item, reel and frame references hold S7, Bute X.6(12) and any Vaughan or Bute deeds for Llandough-juxta-Penarth?
  • Will the Library confirm or deny whether it holds a 1660s lease or a later recital of one?
  • What does a copy of only the relevant folios of S7 cost?

Under section 8 any written request that gives a name and describes the information sought is a valid request, whichever team receives it, so the five held or not held questions fall within FOIA; an archive search team answering in service style is common practice and not a refusal in itself.
  • The earlier Companies House reply of 2 November 2026 set out a general disposal programme: paper files of England and Wales companies dissolved between 1992 and 2003 transferred to The National Archives or destroyed from July 2024, and microfiche disposed of or transferred by early 2024. Both companies were dissolved inside that window, so on Companies House's own dates the disposal came before the first preservation request of 10 October 2026. The replies are consistent with that programme.
  • The 20-year rule cited concerns public inspection of the register. It explains why records are no longer open to view; it is a different matter from the physical destruction of paper and microfiche, which is a records-management decision. The replies run the two together.
  • Conveyances of land are not generally among the documents filed at Companies House, so silence on the 1926 and 1938 conveyances may reflect that they were never held there; the replies do not say so.
